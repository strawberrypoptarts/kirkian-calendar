The Kirkian Calendar

What is the Kirkian Calendar?

The Kirkian Calendar is an alternative calendar system that begins on September 10, 2025. This date marks the beginning of the calendar, designated as year 0 AK (After Kirk). The Kirkian Calendar provides a unique way to track time with its own months, years, and traditions.

Year Numbering

The Kirkian Calendar uses year numbering that starts from the epoch date. The abbreviation AK stands for After Kirk.

  • September 10, 2025 = 0 AK, Kirkfall 1
  • September 10, 2026 = 1 AK, Kirkfall 1
  • September 10, 2027 = 2 AK, Kirkfall 1
  • September 10, 2028 = 3 AK, Kirkfall 1

Each Kirkian year runs from September 10 through September 9 of the following Gregorian year.

The 10 Months

The Kirkian year contains exactly 10 months. It has 365 days in normal years and 366 days in leap years, with no separate year-end days.

How Gregorian Dates Correspond

The Kirkian Calendar is anchored to the Gregorian Calendar. The first day of each Kirkian year (Kirkfall 1) always falls on September 10 in the Gregorian Calendar. Because the Kirkian year has 365 days (or 366 in leap years), the correspondence between Kirkian and Gregorian dates shifts slightly each year.

The conversion is mathematically exact — it counts the total number of days between any date and the epoch (September 10, 2025) and uses that to determine the correct Kirkian year, month, and day.

Key Dates

  • Kirkian New Year — September 10 (Kirkfall 1)
  • Founding Day — September 10 (anniversary of the calendar)
  • Last Day of iQuarters — September 9 (final day of the Kirkian year)

Leap Years

The Kirkian Calendar does not have its own leap year system. Instead, it follows the Gregorian Calendar's leap year rules, so the year anchors stay aligned: every Kirkian year begins on September 10 of the corresponding Gregorian year.

When the Gregorian Calendar includes a February 29, the Kirkian year containing that date has 366 days instead of 365. Because there are no separate year-end days, the extra (leap) day extends the final month, iQuarters, from 41 days to 42 days in leap years. Months 1–9 always have exactly 36 days.
